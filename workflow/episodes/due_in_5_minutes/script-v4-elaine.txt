IGNITE x SOL: "DUE IN 5 MINUTES"
Script v4 (shooting script)

(Elaine's script, pasted 2026-11-26, kept word for word. Production notes are in script.txt.)

====================================================================
SPECS
====================================================================
Format:      Vertical 9:16 (1080 x 1920). This is the master.
Length:      About 40 seconds
Narrator:    Arthur (only voice). Sol is silent and answers by acting.
Course:      Scientific Research & Data Analysis (developer: Daniya Shivapur)
Ending:      Sol nods on the last clip and holds.
Captions:    Must match the narration below word for word. Any take that
             differs gets regenerated, not cut around.
Pacing:      Narration is never sped up. If a take runs long, extend the
             clip or cut Tip 5's clip slightly, don't speed the voice.

Framing rule for every clip: keep Sol and the main action in the center
third of the frame, and keep captions out of the bottom fifth and the
right edge (platform buttons cover those areas). This also lets you
reframe to 16:9 and 1:1 later if you run Google Ads.


====================================================================
THE STORY IN ONE LINE
====================================================================
Sol's science project is due in five minutes. Arthur gives him five
tips, Sol runs around the house making one piece of the poster per tip,
and in the final shot every piece is on the finished poster.

Opening question: Will the project be ready in time?
Ending answer:    "So, is it ready now?" Sol nods.


====================================================================
CHARACTER NOTES
====================================================================
- Sol: flame mascot, no mouth, no eyebrows. Emotion comes from the
  eyes, body (hops, leans, nods) and flame color.
- Use ONLY expressions from expression sheet v3, by exact name:
    Normal / Blink / Looking to the side / Looking up /
    > < squeezed shut / O O surprised / Sparkles /
    Surprised, mid-bounce / Worried / Shocked: worried + O O /
    Worried, from the side / Shocked, other side
- Flame color arc (this is the real storytelling):
    Orange (start) -> snaps PURPLE at the shock (0:03) -> stays purple
    through the setup -> fades back to orange tip by tip -> one quick
    purple flicker at Tip 5 -> full, brightest orange with Sparkles at
    the end.
- "Shocked" is used only once (Clip 1) so the hook hits hard.
- Goggles: either Sol wears them in EVERY clip (to match the course
  card art) or in none. Never some clips and not others.
- Props: one simple prop per clip. Actions stick to tap, nudge, point,
  lean in, hop, nod.


====================================================================
LOCK THIS FIRST: THE FINISHED POSTER (seen in Clip 8)
====================================================================
Design and lock the finished poster before generating any clips. Every
piece in Clips 3 to 7 has to match it exactly.

Trifold poster, left to right:
  TOP (across the whole poster):  "Does salt make ice melt faster?"
  LEFT PANEL:   "Changed: amount of salt"
                "Measured: minutes to melt"
                Three small labels: "Trial 1" "Trial 2" "Trial 3"
  CENTER PANEL: Simple bar graph (2 bars: plain ice vs salted ice,
                salted bar is shorter = melts faster)
  RIGHT PANEL:  "What went wrong" page (from the notebook)

====================================================================
FULL NARRATION / CAPTION TEXT (word for word)
====================================================================
Sol, what are you doing? Wait, your science project's due in five
minutes?

Okay, here are five things that'll save it.

First, write your question at the top so judges know what you were
testing.

Second, say the one thing you changed and what you measured.

Third, say how many times you ran it, because one try could just be
luck.

Fourth, turn your numbers into a graph, since nobody reads a wall of
numbers.

And fifth, write down what went wrong, because real scientists always
do.

Ignite's free Scientific Research and Data Analysis course teaches you
how real research works, from collecting data to analyzing it. So, is
it ready now?

Word count: 112 (about 37 seconds of speech at a natural pace)
