DIALOGUE AND WHAT SOL DOES (read this first)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
01  (no words yet) The camera flies in from the ceiling of the bathroom, down over the curtain rod and into the
      tub. Sol is in the shower, swaying, SINGING (music notes). The curtain's hem creeps in and boops his side.
      He jolts, STARTLED (!). Then he turns on the curtain, ANGRY (red shade), with two huffy bounces.
    Narrator: "Sol, why are you glaring at the shower curtain?"
      Still glaring, he glances up at the voice.
02    His answer: he CATFIGHTS it. Eyes squeezed shut (> <), leaning away, both little arm nubs slapping at
      the curtain as fast as they go. It flutters back under the pats, then swings straight in and boops him
      anyway. He freezes, eyes wide (O O).
    Narrator: "Oh, it creeps toward you when the water's on."
      Sol looks up. On "creeps toward you" he gives the curtain a NERVOUS sideways look (sweat drop). He nods once.
03  Narrator: "Watch the spray. Falling water drags air down with it."
      Over Sol's shoulder: he tilts, curious. The camera rushes past him up to the shower head, then falls
      with the water. Pale steam puffs beside the drops get dragged down with them to the tub floor.
04  Narrator: "That air loops back around like a little sideways whirlwind."
      The camera rides along the floor with the puffs to Sol. They sweep up past his face (AMAZED) and back
      along the top. The camera peels away to the side and the whole thing shows: one big slow wheel of steam.
      Sol follows a puff round with his whole body, one circle, and ends DIZZY (spiral eyes), wobbling.
05  Narrator: "The middle of a whirlwind is low pressure, so it pushes on the curtain a little less."
      CLOSE-UP. The camera corkscrews into the middle of the wheel, right against the curtain, and holds
      still. The puffs circle; the calm middle is empty. The cloth behind it eases toward us. Sol is not here.
06  Narrator: "The room outside pushes like normal, so the room wins and shoves the curtain in."
      The camera starts outside the shower, low on the bathroom rug: calm, a rubber duck on the tub rim. It
      glides to the curtain, cranes up and over the rod and looks down into the tub. Sol is watching the hem
      creep toward him, NERVOUS. He tries to run for it (PANIC), but the tub floor is wet: his legs run in
      place, going nowhere, then shoot out from under him. He slips backward and flops flat on his back at the
      curtain's hem. On "shoves" the hem swings in and boops him where he lies. Knocked out (X X eyes).
07  Narrator: "It's not creeping up on you. It's getting pushed. Got it?"
      Sol pops back up onto his feet with a wobble, the hem resting against him. THINKING (dots). On
      "getting pushed": IDEA (light bulb). He
      bumps the curtain away with his side; it swings out and settles back on him, and this time he lets it,
      SMUG, puffed up. The camera swings round to face him. He looks up, EXCITED, and nods twice (HAPPY eyes).

HOW IT IS FILMED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Set: a small sunny bathroom, new for this episode. A white tub alcove with pale tiles, the shower head on the
  far wall, a pleated curtain along the left side on a steel rod with rings, a round window, a shelf with two
  bottles and a rubber duck. Beyond the curtain: cream wall, rug, sink with a round mirror, towel, potted
  plant. Two more ducks (tub rim, tub floor) and a bar of soap. The ducks and soap never move.
Fixed places: Sol stands mid-tub near the camera end; the spray lands at the far end; the curtain's hem bows
  in the most just beyond Sol, where it can touch his side.
The air is shown with steam puffs: the same 40 pale puffs in every clip (same seed), modelled in the skeleton
  in their real colour so the painter cannot restyle them. The curtain is one sheet, same colour and pleats
  in every clip; it moves only by bowing in, by Sol's shove, and with a small ripple while the water runs.
Camera: every clip travels. The big move happens at the start or end of the clip and settles while the science
  has to be read (clip 05 holds still for its last four seconds). No two neighbouring clips share a framing.
Sol's faces: all from assets/sol-3d/eyes (EXPR names in capitals above). Every swap goes through emotion_at, so
  it comes with the bounce. Floating marks (notes, !, dots, bulb, dashes) sit on a flat plane in front of him,
  so each one is timed for when the camera is roughly in front of him. The red shade is on only while he is
  ANGRY. Each paint pass gets a crop of the expression sheet showing just the faces that clip uses; the painter
  has not been tried on the newer faces, so clip 01 is painted first as the test.
Sol never reaches with an arm. The catfight is not a reach: his nubs stay their own short length and flap up
  and down in place, alternating, about six slaps a second. The curtain is already against his side, so the
  nubs pat it where they are. Every other touch is a bump or lean with his side, nubs tucked.
The gags, each used once: singing in the shower (01), the catfight (02), going dizzy from following the air
  round (04), running in place then slipping and flopping (06).
His feet stay on the tub floor, and jolts are a planted squash-and-spring, not a hop. The one exception is the
  slip in clip 06: his feet fly up as he goes over, and he lands flat on the tub floor, in contact with it and
  his shadow, and stays there to the end of the clip.
Skeleton: skeleton/clips.py with rig.py, rendered headless by render_clip.py at 24 fps.
Paint: Gemini Omni Flash 1.2, mode edit, 720p. One frame of clip 01 is the style reference for the other six,
  with Sol's turnaround sheet. About 40.3 s, so about 91 credits.
Edit: edit.json with scripts/episode_edit.py; caption times from the transcript's word times.
Checks: gray frames before paint; check_faces.py on each painted clip (planned faces will be flagged at their
  planned times); check_narration.py on the cut; frame_review.py on the final cut, every frame looked at.

Accuracy: the "shower-curtain effect". David Schmidt's 2001 computer simulation found that the spray drives a
  vortex whose axis points at the curtain, with a low-pressure middle that draws the curtain in. It happens
  with cold water too, so rising hot air is at most a helper. Physicists still argue about how much each cause
  matters; the script states the vortex explanation as the answer.
