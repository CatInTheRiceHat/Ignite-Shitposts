DIALOGUE AND WHAT SOL DOES (read this first)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
01  Narrator: "Sol, why are you glaring at the shower curtain?"
      Sol stands in the tub with the shower running. The curtain is bowed in beside him. He leans toward it
      with two huffy little bounces, then glances up at the voice.
      His answer: he shoves the curtain away with his side. It swings out, swings straight back and boops him.
      He jolts (O O eyes) and hops away from it.
    Narrator: "Oh, it creeps toward you when the water's on."
      Sol looks up, glances back at the curtain, and nods once.
02  Narrator: "Watch the spray. Falling water drags air down with it."
      Sol turns to the shower and tilts, curious. CLOSE on the spray: pale steam puffs beside the falling drops
      get pulled down with them, all the way to the tub floor.
03  Narrator: "That air loops back around like a little sideways whirlwind."
      Seen from the side: the puffs run along the tub floor, rise at Sol's end, drift back along the top and
      get pulled down by the spray again. One big slow wheel of steam. Sol follows a puff round with his whole
      body, one slow circle, and wobbles.
04  Narrator: "The middle of a whirlwind is low pressure, so it pushes on the curtain a little less."
      CLOSE-UP. The middle of the steam wheel, right against the curtain. The puffs circle; the calm middle
      is empty. The cloth behind the middle eases toward us. Sol is not in this shot.
05  Narrator: "The room outside pushes like normal, so the room wins and shoves the curtain in."
      From high above the curtain rod: the quiet bathroom on one side, the tub with Sol on the other, the
      curtain between them. On "shoves" the hem swings in and boops Sol's side. He jolts (O O eyes).
06  Narrator: "It's not creeping up on you. It's getting pushed. Got it?"
      Close on the hem resting against Sol. On "pushed" he bumps it away with his side; it swings out and
      settles back against him, and this time he lets it and puffs up proudly. He looks up (sparkle eyes for
      a beat) and nods twice.

HOW IT IS FILMED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Set: a small sunny bathroom, new for this episode. A white tub alcove with pale tiles, the shower head on the
  far wall, a pleated curtain along the left side on a steel rod with rings, a round window, a shelf with two
  bottles and a rubber duck. Beyond the curtain: cream wall, rug, sink with a round mirror, towel, potted
  plant. Two more ducks (tub rim, tub floor) and a bar of soap. The ducks and soap never move.
Fixed places: Sol stands mid-tub near the camera end; the spray lands at the far end; the curtain's hem bows
  in the most just beyond Sol, where it can touch his side.
The air is shown with steam puffs: the same 40 pale puffs in every clip (same seed), modelled in the skeleton
  in their real colour so the painter cannot restyle them. The curtain is one sheet, same colour and pleats
  in every clip; it moves only by bowing in, by Sol's shove, and with a small ripple while the water runs.
Sol never reaches with an arm: every touch is a bump or lean with his side, arm nubs tucked.
His feet stay on the tub floor except the one hop in clip 1.
Skeleton: skeleton/clips.py with rig.py, rendered headless by render_clip.py. Sol is the approved 3D model
  with the separate-eyes rig; every expression change goes through emotion_at (the bounce).
Paint: Gemini Omni Flash 1.2, mode edit, 720p. Clip 01 first; one of its frames is the style reference for
  the other five, with Sol's turnaround sheet.
Edit: edit.json with scripts/episode_edit.py; caption times from the transcript's word times.
Checks: gray frames before paint; check_faces.py on each painted clip; check_narration.py on the cut;
  frame_review.py on the final cut, every frame looked at.

Accuracy: the "shower-curtain effect". David Schmidt's 2001 computer simulation found that the spray drives a
  vortex whose axis points at the curtain, with a low-pressure middle that draws the curtain in. It happens
  with cold water too, so rising hot air is at most a helper. Physicists still argue about how much each cause
  matters; the script states the vortex explanation as the answer.
