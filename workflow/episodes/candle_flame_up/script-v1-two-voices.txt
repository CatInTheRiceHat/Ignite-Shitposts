THE IDEA IN ONE LINE
A flame points up because the hot air it makes rises and cooler air slides in
underneath. Take away "up" (in orbit) and the flame turns into a little blue ball.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DIALOGUE ONLY (read this first)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
01  Sol:      "Why does a candle flame always point up, even when I tip it?"
02  Narrator: "Because the flame heats the air around it. Hot air is lighter, so it
               rises, and you can see it spin this paper spiral."
03  Narrator: "Cooler air slides in at the bottom to replace it. That rising stream
               pulls the flame into a teardrop."
04  Sol:      "What if there's no up?"
    Narrator: "In orbit, hot air doesn't rise. The flame just sits there as a little
               blue ball."
05  Sol:      "So up is just where the hot air goes."
    Narrator: "Exactly."

HOW EVERY CLIP GETS FILMED (same recipe for all five)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Voices first. Record the Arthur and Sol takes, trim dead air, measure the real
   cue times. Clip lengths below get adjusted to those before any animation.
2. Blender skeleton. Build the shot in gray: a flat gray Sol stand-in with the
   mascot silhouette, arm and foot nubs, and two dark eye ovals placed at the
   Mascot.png spacing (so the painter has no room to spread the eyes apart), plus
   gray blocks for the props. Animate the camera in Blender too (Elaine, 2026-11-25:
   dynamic camera angles), so the paint pass follows a planned move instead of
   inventing one. Key every pose and camera position on twos: constant
   interpolation, a new pose every 2 frames at 30 fps, no in-betweens, no motion blur.
   Render one gray 1080x1920 clip per scene.
3. Higgsfield paint pass. Gemini Omni Flash 1.2, mode edit, the gray clip as the
   video reference, Mascot.png as the image reference, prompt = the scene's setting
   and lighting below plus the shared gouache and motion blocks. No stills made in
   between. Last quote was 13 credits per clip; balance is 361.75.
4. Conform. The last render came back at 24 fps with no held frames, so the on-twos
   cadence gets enforced in the edit (30 fps, every state held exactly 2 frames,
   1x speed, no blending) and checked frame by frame.
5. Review the actual footage against this script: eyes, mouthless face, arms, feet,
   no particles, upward gaze during Narrator lines. Then captions, voices, music.

Room continuity: clips 01, 02, 03 and 05 are the same desk. Each paint pass is a
separate job, so the room could drift between them. Plan: paint 01 first, and once
you accept it, pass a frame from that clip as an extra reference for 02, 03 and 05.

CLIP 01 — Sol tips the candle — about 4.0s
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Teaching purpose: show the puzzle. The candle leans, the flame doesn't.
Setting: a cozy wooden desk at night by a frosty window. One short cream candle in
  a small brass dish holder, a stack of two books, a pencil cup, a paper spiral
  hanging on a thread above the candle (it matters in clip 02). Warm candle pool on
  the desk, cool blue window light behind. No floating particles.
Framing: medium shot at desk level, camera slightly above Sol's eye height.
  Camera move: starts wider and a little to Sol's left, then pushes in and arcs
  about 20 degrees around toward the candle as he tips it, ending close on the
  leaning candle and upright flame with Sol still in frame. Positions below are
  for the END of the move.
  Sol at x=36%, feet on the desk at y=72%, height about 26%. Candle at x=62%, flame
  around y=48%. Lower fifth quiet for captions.
First frame: Sol stands three-quarter toward the candle, both eyes visible, arms
  low. Candle upright, flame a steady upright teardrop.
Action: Sol reaches the near arm to the brass dish and pushes it once (0.4–1.4s) so
  the candle leans about 25 degrees away from him. The flame stays straight up the
  whole time, hinged at the wick. Sol leans his own body the same way to compare,
  with a small tip wobble that settles by 2.4s, then holds, looking at the flame.
  One brief blink allowed.
Speaker attention: only Sol speaks. No upward look needed.
Sol: "Why does a candle flame always point up, even when I tip it?"
Last frame: candle leaning, flame vertical, Sol leaning with one arm still on the
  dish, feet flat.
Next cut: hard cut to a close-up of the same flame, upright again.
Blender: 120 frames. Gray Sol, gray cylinder candle on a dish that pivots at its
  base edge, a small teardrop for the flame parented to the wick but with rotation
  locked to world-up. Spiral hangs still.

CLIP 02 — Hot air spins the spiral — about 7.5s
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Teaching purpose: hot air rises, and here is proof you can see.
Setting: same desk, candle upright. Sol is out of frame.
Framing: close-up, low angle looking slightly up. Flame at x=50%, y=64%. The paper
  spiral (cream paper, orange underside) hangs above it, filling y=18%–46%. Dark
  blue window behind so the pale paper reads.
  Camera move: starts tight on the flame alone, then cranes straight up and tilts
  with the rising air to find the spiral just as it starts turning (about 2.5s),
  and ends with flame low and spiral filling the top half. Positions above are
  for the END of the move.
First frame: flame steady. Spiral hanging completely still.
Action: 0–2.5s the spiral stays still while a soft warm shimmer builds above the
  flame tip. At about 2.5s (on "so it rises") the spiral starts to turn, slowly at
  first, then up to a steady spin by 4.5s and keeps going to the end. The thread
  twists a little. Flame flickers only at its tip, base fixed on the wick.
Speaker attention: Sol is off screen, so no gaze cue.
Narrator: "Because the flame heats the air around it. Hot air is lighter, so it
  rises, and you can see it spin this paper spiral."
Last frame: spiral mid-spin, flame steady.
Next cut: cut down to the base of the flame.
Blender: 226 frames. Gray teardrop flame, gray helix strip for the spiral with a
  stepped Z rotation that eases in from frame 76. No Sol.

CLIP 03 — Cool air slides in underneath — about 6.5s
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Teaching purpose: the other half of the loop. Air comes in low, goes out high, and
  that stream is what stretches the flame.
Setting: same desk. An incense stick in a tiny clay holder stands a hand-width to
  the left of the candle, giving off one thin pale thread of smoke. Sol is out of
  frame.
Framing: tight close-up at wick height, level camera. Wick at x=56%, y=66%. Incense
  tip at x=24%, y=62%. Room above the flame to see the smoke get carried up.
  Camera move: starts on the incense tip, slides sideways with the smoke thread to
  the base of the flame, then tilts up a little as the smoke gets swept upward.
  Positions above are for the END of the move.
First frame: the smoke thread rises straight up from the incense tip, not yet near
  the candle.
Action: 0–2.5s the smoke thread bends sideways, low, toward the base of the flame
  (on "slides in at the bottom"). 2.5–4.5s it reaches the base, turns the corner and
  gets swept straight up along the side of the flame. As it does, the flame visibly
  stretches a little taller and narrower into a clean teardrop. Holds that state to
  the end with the smoke flowing along the same path.
Speaker attention: Sol is off screen, so no gaze cue.
Narrator: "Cooler air slides in at the bottom to replace it. That rising stream
  pulls the flame into a teardrop."
Last frame: smoke path in an L shape, in low and out high. Tall teardrop flame.
Next cut: hard cut to space.
Blender: 196 frames. Gray teardrop with a stepped scale change (wider to taller),
  a gray curve for the smoke thread whose control points are keyed along the L path.
Note: the smoke is incense smoke from its own stick. It is not coming off Sol.

CLIP 04 — No up, blue ball — about 8.0s
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Teaching purpose: the test. Remove rising air and the teardrop goes away.
Setting: inside a small space-station module. Cream padded walls, a round window
  with the blue curve of Earth, a floating pencil and a floating paper spiral
  drifting slowly. On a workbench, a clear sealed experiment box holding a short
  candle. Cool white cabin light, the flame adding a faint blue glow on the glass.
Framing: medium shot, camera slightly above Sol. Sol stands on the workbench at
  x=30%, feet at y=70% tucked under a small foot strap (he stays planted, no
  floating), height about 24%. Experiment box at x=64%, flame at y=50%. Window
  upper right.
  Camera move: a slow weightless drift, orbiting about 30 degrees around the
  experiment box with a slight roll, pushing in on the flame as it turns into the
  blue ball. Sol stays in frame the whole time. Positions above are for the
  START of the move.
First frame: Sol faces the box, near arm half raised toward it. The candle flame is
  a normal small orange teardrop that is just starting to shrink.
Action: 0–1.6s Sol finishes a small questioning arm lift toward the box while he
  speaks. 1.6–2.4s he tilts his whole body back to look up at the Narrator point
  above camera and settles, arms lowering. From 2.4s to about 5.0s the flame rounds
  off and dims from orange teardrop to a small soft blue ball sitting on the wick,
  then stays that way. Pencil and spiral keep drifting slowly. Sol keeps a gentle
  listening idle, looking up, to the end.
Speaker attention: Sol's line 0.1–1.5s. Gaze up ready by 2.4s. Narrator 2.6–7.6s
  (provisional). Sol keeps looking up for the whole Narrator line.
Sol: "What if there's no up?"
Narrator: "In orbit, hot air doesn't rise. The flame just sits there as a little
  blue ball."
Last frame: blue ball flame, Sol tilted up, feet under the strap, arms low.
Next cut: hard cut back to the desk.
Blender: 240 frames. Gray Sol with a stepped body pitch for the look up (whole body
  and face together, eyes not reshaped). Flame proxy morphs from teardrop to sphere.
  Two small drifting props on slow stepped paths.
Accuracy note: real candle tests in orbit were done in sealed boxes like this, and
  the flames came out round, dim and blue because fresh air only reaches them slowly.

CLIP 05 — Sol gets it — about 5.0s
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Teaching purpose: answer the opening question with the same action that asked it.
Setting and framing: same desk as clip 01. Candle upright, spiral spinning steadily
  above it.
  Camera move: starts close on Sol's arm and the candle dish, then pulls back and
  rises as his other arm sweeps up, revealing the spinning spiral above, and ends
  on a medium shot holding Sol, candle and spiral together.
First frame: Sol beside the candle, near arm already on the brass dish.
Action: 0–1.2s Sol tips the candle again, the flame stays vertical. 1.2–2.6s he
  lifts his other arm and sweeps it once straight up along the path from the flame
  to the spinning spiral, small tip wobble, settle. 2.8–3.4s he tilts his body up
  toward the Narrator point and holds a gentle listening idle through "Exactly."
Speaker attention: Sol's line 0.1–2.6s. Gaze up ready by 3.4s. Narrator 3.6–4.4s
  (provisional). Hold the picture to 5.0s.
Sol: "So up is just where the hot air goes."
Narrator: "Exactly."
Last frame: candle leaning, flame up, spiral spinning, Sol looking up, feet flat.
End: stop after the full "Exactly." No end card unless you want one.
Blender: 150 frames. Reuse the clip 01 scene file, new pose and camera keys.
