DIALOGUE AND WHAT SOL DOES (read this first)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
01  Narrator: "What are you doing, Sol?"
      Sol is wiggling a candle back and forth. The flame stays straight up.
      He looks up and holds the candle tipped, showing the Narrator.
    Narrator: "Ohh, you must be wondering why the flame is still standing straight up."
      Sol nods.
02  Narrator: "It's the air. The flame heats it, and hot air rises. Slide that paper
               spiral over it."
      Sol watches the flame, then shuffles over and pushes the wire stand so the
      paper spiral hangs above the flame. It starts to spin. He throws his arms up.
    Narrator: "See? The rising air spins it."
03  Narrator: "Down low, cooler air slides in to replace it. That upward stream
               stretches the flame into a teardrop."
      On "Down low" Sol ducks to desk level. Incense smoke bends in toward the base
      of the flame, turns the corner and shoots up. Sol's gaze follows it up while
      the flame stretches taller.
04  Narrator: "But what if there's no up? In orbit, hot air doesn't rise, so the
               flame becomes a little blue ball."
      Space station. Sol looks up and shrugs, turns to the candle in its glass box,
      watches the flame round off into a blue ball, leans in and touches the glass.
05  Narrator: "So for a flame, up is just where the hot air goes. Got it?"
      Sol lifts the dish by its handle so the candle leans once more. He lets go and
      raises that same arm to point up, and the candle drops back flat. He looks up
      at the Narrator and nods twice.

HOW IT IS FILMED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Narrator takes first; clip lengths come from the real takes.
2. Gray Blender skeleton per clip: skeleton/rig.py (round gumdrop Sol with close-set
   eyes, arm nubs, feet; candle, stand, spiral, desk, space set) and skeleton/clips.py
   (poses, timing, camera move). Everything keyed on twos with stepped keys and
   verified: each frame pair is pixel-identical. Output skeleton/0N-skeleton-v2.mp4.
3. Higgsfield paint pass: Gemini Omni Flash 1.2, mode edit, the gray clip as the video
   reference, Mascot.png as the image reference. Draft pass at 720p (2.25 credits per
   second, about 83 credits for the whole video; 1080p would be about 120).
   Clip 01 is painted first and one of its frames is passed to 02, 03 and 05 so the
   desk room matches.
4. Conform each painted clip to 30 fps on twos (the model returns 24 fps with no
   holds), lay the Narrator takes, captions and music, export.
5. Review the real footage against this script.

Sol and the Narrator: Sol looks UP when he is being spoken to or answering, and looks

CLIP 01 — "What are you doing, Sol?" — 7.0s
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Teaching purpose: the puzzle. The candle leans, the flame does not.
Set: cozy desk at night, frosty window. Candle in a brass dish. A wire stand with the
  paper spiral stands behind and to the right, spiral still.
Camera: starts wide and left, pushes in and arcs right to a medium shot of Sol and the
  candle (0-3s), then keeps creeping in.
Action: 0.1-1.35 Sol tips the candle, lets it back, tips it again. 1.45-2.15 he tilts
  back and looks up, candle still tipped, far arm popping up once. Holds, listening.
  6.4-6.95 one nod.
Narrator: "What are you doing, Sol?" 0.15-1.35
Narrator: "Ohh, you must be wondering why the flame is still standing straight up." 2.5-6.35

CLIP 02 — hot air spins the spiral — 9.2s
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Teaching purpose: hot air rises, and you can see it turn something.
Set: same desk. Sol now on the right, next to the stand.
Camera: starts tight on the flame, then pulls back and cranes up (2.6-5.8s) to hold
  the spiral, candle and Sol together.
Action: Sol watches the flame, eyes drifting up on "hot air rises". 4.5-6.0 he
  shuffles left pushing the stand until the spiral hangs over the flame. 6.1 the spiral
  starts turning, full speed by 7.1. 6.9-7.7 both arms up, looking at the spiral.
Narrator: "It's the air. The flame heats it, and hot air rises. Slide that paper spiral
  over it." 0.15-6.2
Narrator: "See? The rising air spins it." 6.6-8.9

CLIP 03 — cool air slides in underneath — 7.6s
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Teaching purpose: the other half of the loop. In low, out high, and that stream is what
  stretches the flame.
Set: same desk. An incense stick in a small dish stands left of the candle, giving off
  one thin thread of smoke. Sol stands left of the incense. Spiral spinning above.
Camera: slides right from the incense toward the flame (0.2-3.2s), then pulls back and
  tilts up as the smoke climbs.
Action: 0.35-1.0 Sol ducks low. 0.6-2.8 the smoke bends sideways toward the base of the
  flame. 2.9-4.5 it turns the corner and streams up the side of the flame. 4.0-5.5 the
  flame stretches taller and Sol straightens, following the smoke up.
Narrator: "Down low, cooler air slides in to replace it. That upward stream stretches
  the flame into a teardrop." 0.2-7.1
Note: the smoke is from the incense stick, not from Sol.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CLIP 04 — no up, blue ball — 7.6s
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Teaching purpose: the test. Take away rising air and the teardrop goes away.
Set: small space-station module, round window with Earth, a floating pencil and paper
  spiral. Sol stands on a workbench with his feet under a strap. A short candle sits in
  a clear sealed box beside him.
Camera: slow weightless drift, orbiting right and pushing in.
Action: 0.4-1.5 Sol looks up and shrugs. 1.6-2.4 he turns to the box. 2.6-5.0 the flame
  rounds off from orange teardrop to a small dim blue ball. 5.0-5.8 Sol leans in and
  rests an arm on the glass.
Narrator: "But what if there's no up? In orbit, hot air doesn't rise, so the flame
  becomes a little blue ball." 0.2-6.9
Accuracy: real candle tests in orbit were done in sealed boxes and gave round, dim, blue
  flames.
