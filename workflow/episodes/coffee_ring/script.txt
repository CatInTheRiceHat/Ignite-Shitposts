------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DIALOGUE AND WHAT SOL DOES (read this first)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
01  Narrator: "Sol, what are you staring at?"
      Close on a dried coffee ring on a white kitchen counter. Sol leans over it and taps toward it twice,
      then looks up.
    Narrator: "Oh, you want to know why coffee dries into a ring, not a blob."
      Sol nods.
02  Narrator: "Watch a fresh drop."
      Sol hops over and nudges his coffee mug. The mug rocks once and one drop hops out onto the counter.
03  Narrator: "It dries fastest at its edge. But the edge is stuck to the counter, so it can't shrink inward."
      CLOSE-UP. The drop fills the frame. Vapour rises from its rim only. Its outline stays put while its
      top slowly sinks. Sol watches from behind.
04  Narrator: "So water rushes out from the middle to refill it, and it carries every coffee speck with it."
      CLOSE-UP, lower. The dark specks inside the drop drift outward toward the rim.
05  Narrator: "Then the water dries away, and the specks stay, piled at the edge."
      CLOSE-UP from straight above. The last specks reach the rim, the water thins away, the ring is left.
06  Narrator: "So the ring isn't a mess. It's every speck, delivered to the edge. Got it?"
      Close on the new ring, pull back to Sol. He points at it, looks up, nods twice.

Accuracy: the coffee-ring effect (Deegan et al., Nature 1997). Evaporation is fastest at the pinned edge of
  the drop, which drives an outward flow that carries suspended particles to the edge.
