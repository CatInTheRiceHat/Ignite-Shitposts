    Narrator: "You tried, but nothing stained it."
      Partway through the line, Sol bounces upright (NORMAL), looks up at the Narrator, and tilts curiously
      down at his sunlit gray tee.

      CLOSE-UP, steep three-quarter macro looking down on the dry light-gray weave of Sol's shirt (upper half
      of frame). Bright golden-yellow light arrows stream down onto the top cotton yarns and open air gaps and
      scatter sharply straight back out at full brightness. Sol is not here.
05  Narrator: "Water bends light almost like cotton, so less scatters and light travels deeper."
      CLOSE-UP, low side-on cross-section sliding into the wet weave (upper half of frame). Clear glossy water
      fills every gap between the exact same light-gray yarns and forms a smooth top layer. Golden arrows pass
      into the water with very little scattering at the top and travel deep down among the dyed gray fibers,
      starting to dim toward muted amber. Sol is not here.
06    (no words yet) Back on the sidewalk: Sol is squished way down into a comical deep crouch-squash on
      planted feet, squinting ridiculously close at the dark wet patch on his own chest (THINKING, dots).
    Narrator: "Staring won't dry it."
      Caught in the act by the older gentleman's cheeky quip, he pops slightly upright with a sheepish blush
      (BASHFUL), glancing up at the voice and back down at his wet shirt.
07  Narrator: "Water's underside reflects light back, which is total internal reflection."
      CLOSE-UP, tight low upward-angled cross-section slice inside the wet weave framing the smooth glossy
      underside of the top water surface above the exact same light-gray yarns (upper half of frame). Golden-
      to-amber arrows rising back upward from the submerged fibers hit the smooth water underside at shallow
      angles and reflect sharply back down into the weave again and again, thinning and dimming from bright
      gold to muted amber to faint bronze. Sol is not here.
08  Narrator: "Extra trips let dyed fibers absorb more light."
      Opens straight-on facing Sol wearing his plain gray tee, comparing the dry light-gray half of his chest
      beside the dark wet patch on his front (upper half of frame). Sol holds completely still facing the
      camera (CONTENT) while four bright golden arrows scatter boldly off the dry cloth and only one thin faint

      stars).
09  Narrator: "The cloth never changed color, and less light reaches your eyes because more is absorbed."
      Camera orbits cleanly around the outside of a terracotta marigold planter down to face Sol at eye level,
      then eases slowly in on him for the rest of the clip. Standing tall (NORMAL) in his plain gray tee with
      the dark wet patch on its front, he tilts his compact body gently upward and listens through the entire
      line, glancing briefly down at his shirt on "never changed color" and right back up, with one quick clean
      blink. Once the line ends, he gives one big bouncy satisfied nod.
10  Narrator: "Next time you get a water stain on gray clothes, don't let it land anywhere embarrassing."
      CLOSURE SCENE (new moment on the same sunny street corner). Sol is now wearing ONLY a simple one-piece
      pair of plain light-gray pants (same gray as the shirt, sitting low on his round body under his face with

Accuracy: wet porous fabrics darken through optical index matching and internal light trapping (Lekner & Dorf,
  Applied Optics 1988). How strongly light scatters at a boundary grows with the refractive-index contrast
  between the two materials: dry cotton fibers (refractive index n ~ 1.53) surrounded by air (n ~ 1.00) have a
  large optical mismatch, producing strong Fresnel reflection and diffuse surface scattering right off the
  outer fibers back to the eye. When water (n ~ 1.33) fills those inter-fiber air spaces, water's refractive
  index is much closer to cotton's than air's is, so outer fiber surfaces scatter far less light back and
  incoming light travels deeper into the dyed weave. Light scattered back toward the outside then meets the
  smooth water-to-air underside and, at incidence angles beyond the critical angle (~49 degrees from the normal,
  i.e., shallow angles to the surface), undergoes total internal reflection back into the cloth. Every extra
  trip past the submerged dyed fibers increases absorption by the gray dye, lowering the diffuse reflectance
  returning to your eyes. Simplified here: we omit the term refractive index, numerical indices (1.00, 1.33,
  ~1.53), Fresnel's equations, and the ~49-degree critical angle in the spoken narration; show a handful of
  smooth 2D arrows rather than dense random diffuse scattering; and skip how deeper path-length absorption also
  boosts color saturation on brightly dyed fabrics (neutral gray simply appears darker). Still variable across
  real fabrics: the relative share of reduced surface scattering versus total internal reflection depends on
  fiber chemistry, weave density, yarn twist, and wetness level, and a thick smooth water film can produce
  bright specular glare at grazing viewing angles rather than looking darker.

TITLE: Why Does A Wet Patch Look Darker On A Gray Shirt?

DESCRIPTION: Why does a wet patch look darker on a gray shirt? Sol and the Narrator find out: light scatters more where two materials are very different, so dry air and cotton scatter sunlight straight back out. Water bends light almost like cotton does, so when water fills the gaps, less light scatters at the top and more travels deeper into the cloth. Escaping light then reflects back inside off the water's underside through total internal reflection, and those extra trips let the dyed fibers absorb more light. The cloth never changed color at all, and less light reaches your eyes because more is absorbed, so just be careful where a wet patch lands on gray clothes.
